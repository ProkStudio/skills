# Plots, density and building mix

## Subdivide before building

1. Draw the block (the land between streets) after the roads exist.
2. Set the **depth** of the plot row: 9-13 for town houses, 13-21 for houses with gardens,
   21+ for farms and manors. Keep the depth constant along a row; vary the widths.
3. Cut plot **widths** along the street: 5, 7, 9, 11 in an irregular sequence - never all the
   same, never a smooth progression. Three different widths per row is enough.
4. Leave 1 plot in 6-8 as a yard, garden, well court, stall space or ruin.
5. Mark every plot corner with a marker block (terracotta/wool/concrete) and the street edge
   with a line of the same block. Walk the plan, fix it, then build.

## Density by position

| Position | Coverage | Party walls | Front setback | Floors |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Centre / main street | 80-100% | Yes, continuous rows | 0 (building on the street) | 2-4 |
| Secondary streets | 60-80% | Often | 0-1 | 2-3 |
| Residential mid | 40-60% | Sometimes | 1-3 with a fence and a garden | 1-2 |
| Edge / fringe | 15-30% | No | 3-8, buildings turned at angles | 1-2 |
| Farms | <10% | No | Free-standing groups around a yard | 1-2 + loft |

Drop the height as you leave the centre, and keep the tallest non-landmark buildings on the
main road. The landmark stays the tallest thing in town.

## Terraced rows (the core of any old town)

- Share party walls: each building's side wall is its neighbour's. This is what makes a dense
  core, and it is far faster to build than free-standing houses.
- Vary per unit: width (5/7/9), floor count (2/3), roof ridge height by 1-2, facade material
  within one palette, window rhythm, and the shop-front treatment.
- Step the roofline: never two neighbours with the same ridge height; a 1-2 block step is
  enough.
- Alternate the eaves direction: mostly parallel to the street, with one in four turned gable
  to the street. That single rotation does more for the roofscape than any detailing.
- Jetty the upper floor over the street by 1 block on some units for the medieval look;
  support with corbels (stairs/trapdoors/fence brackets).

## Building mix per district (rule of 60/30/10)

- 60% background buildings: simple, calm, repeated with variation. They exist to make the rest
  read.
- 30% mid buildings: a bay window, a gable, a tower stub, a shop front, a different roof.
- 10% hero buildings: the inn, the church, the town hall, the guild hall - bigger mass, better
  material, more vertical.

Corner plots always get an upgrade: a chamfered corner, a corner entrance, a turret, a shop.
Corners are the most seen part of any block.

## Variation checklist per building

Run this before duplicating anything (see also
`../../minecraft-architecture/references/variation-engine.md`):

- [ ] Footprint differs from both neighbours (width or depth by 2+).
- [ ] Floor count or floor heights differ from at least one neighbour.
- [ ] Roof: type, pitch or ridge direction differs from the neighbour.
- [ ] Palette: same family, different dominant block or accent.
- [ ] Ground floor: door position, shop opening, steps, or a cellar hatch differ.
- [ ] One unique element: chimney position, dormer, sign, balcony, awning, plant.

If a building type must be reused (WorldEdit paste), change at least three of the above and
never paste two copies in the same sight line.

## Yards, gardens and gaps

- Back yards: fenced, with a woodpile, a privy, a garden bed of crops, a compost, laundry on a
  line (string/banners), crates, a shed lean-to.
- Front gardens only in wealthier or rural areas: low wall or fence, flower beds, a path of
  2-wide slabs, a gate.
- Shared courts: 5-9 blocks wide, reached through an arch under a building, with a well, stairs
  to upper flats, and balconies.
- Deliberate empty plots: a burnt-out house with charred blocks, a foundation only (a new build
  with scaffolding), a market space, a tiny chapel, a big tree.

## Survival-specific notes

- Villager mechanics: each villager needs a claimed bed **and** a workstation within pathing
  range; keep beds indoors and reachable, and do not bury workstations behind decoration.
- Iron golems spawn in villages with enough villagers and beds - plan a courtyard where a
  golem is welcome, or keep the count below the threshold if you do not want one.
- Doors, fences and gates: mobs path through open gaps; keep the perimeter closed, gates shut,
  and all walkable blocks at light level 1+.
- Fires: keep fire sources away from wool, planks and thatch in survival, or place them on
  stone with a 1-block gap.
- Farms: leave the field boundaries irregular, but keep the farmland itself in usable
  rectangles so the plot still works for crops.
