# Growth, history and signs of life

A settlement looks real when it looks **built over time by people who needed things**.

## Layered chronology

Decide 3 periods and build them differently:

| Period | Where | Character |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founding core | Around the centre / the oldest crossing | Crooked lanes, small irregular plots, heaviest weathering, oldest materials (cobble, mud, thatch, rough logs), buildings leaning into each other |
| Expansion | Outside the old core, often where a wall stood | More regular plots, better materials (stone bricks, planks, tiles), taller, straighter streets |
| Recent | Edges, along roads, reclaimed industrial land | Regular grid or ribbon, cleanest materials, biggest openings, unfinished corners with scaffolding |

Then add the **traces** of change:

- The old wall line becomes a ring road, a market row or a terrace of houses built into it;
  leave 1-2 wall stubs and a gate tower standing.
- Buildings that grew: an extension in a different material, a floor added with a mismatched
  roof, a shop punched into a house front, a filled-in doorway (different block in the shape
  of an arch).
- A fire scar: a block of newer buildings in a different palette, with one charred ruin left.
- Repairs: patches of a different stone in a wall, a timber prop against a leaning facade, a
  replaced roof section in newer tiles.
- Ruins: a foundation with grass, a stub tower, a broken bridge pier in the river.

## Wear and weathering

- Ground: dirt path and coarse dirt where feet go (doorways, wells, gates, market), grass and
  moss in the quiet corners. Paths are widest at the busiest points.
- Walls: mossy/cracked variants at the base and in the shade, clean at the top of new walls,
  soot near forges and chimneys, oxidised copper on old roofs versus waxed copper on new ones.
- Vegetation reclaiming: vines on north walls, moss carpet in alleys, a sapling in a gutter,
  grass between paving in unused lanes.
- Water: stains under gutters and spouts, puddles in ruts, algae (sea pickles/green blocks) at
  the waterline of quays.

## Signs of daily life (place these in groups of 3)

| Zone | Props |
| --- | --- |
| House front | Bench, woodpile, boot scraper (trapdoor), flower pots, lantern by the door, hanging sign, a broom (fence + trapdoor) |
| Back yard | Laundry line (string/banners), garden beds, compost, privy, chicken coop, barrels, shed |
| Market | Stalls with awnings, crates and barrels, scales, sacks (white/brown blocks), banners, notice board, coin table, a dog on a lead |
| Smithy | Coal pile, water trough, tool rack in item frames, half-made goods, anvil, soot, horseshoe sign |
| Docks | Nets (cobwebs/string), crates, barrels, fish on racks, coils of rope (chains), boats, cranes, gulls (no mobs - use props) |
| Farm | Hay bales, carts, ploughed strip, scarecrow (armor stand), water butt, fence gates left open, animal pens |
| Street | Carts and wheelbarrows, spilled cargo, a broken wheel, lanterns, hitching posts, drain grates (copper grates 1.21) |
| Civic | Steps and plinths, statues, flags, bell, lectern with a book, brazier |
| Construction | Scaffolding, a stack of blocks, a half-built wall, a mortar tub (cauldron), a crane, a fenced-off pit |

Propose props by **story**: who works here, what did they just put down. A cart parked outside
the bakery beats a cart in the middle of the square.

## Population sanity check

Rough plausibility: 4-6 residents per house. So a 20-house village is ~100 people, which needs
a well or two, a mill, a smithy, an inn, a chapel and fields - and one graveyard. Use this to
argue for more non-residential buildings, not to do real maths.

## Growth planning for ongoing worlds

- Leave a stated **expansion direction** with a road stub, a staked-out plot row and a couple
  of "future" foundations.
- Keep a construction site live: scaffolding on one building, a materials dump, a fenced pit.
  A town that is still being built is always more convincing than a finished one.
- Note which district gets richer next, so later additions have a rule to follow.

## Servers and multiplayer

- Plot rows and district palettes double as building rules for other players - publish them on
  a sign board or in a Notion/GitHub doc.
- Reserve the landmark sites and the square edges for good builders, and give newcomers the
  fringe plots where variation matters less.
- Keep performance in mind: entity-heavy decoration (item frames, armor stands, boats) adds up
  fast across dozens of buildings; prefer block-based detail in public areas.

## Final pass

Walk the town at ground level along every street at dusk, then fly the perimeter at eye level
and once from above. Fix in this order: dark spots, blank walls facing public space, identical
neighbours, missing transitions to nature, and any plot that still shows marker blocks.
