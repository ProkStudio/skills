# Layout, roads and public space

## Centre-first method

1. **Centre:** the flattest accessible spot, a crossroads, a river crossing, a harbour head or
   a temple forecourt. Square sizes: hamlet 7x7, village 11x15, town 15x25, city 21x35+.
   Define at least one side with an important facade; never leave a square surrounded by
   back walls.
2. **Landmark:** 1-3 tall elements (tower, keep, pagoda, lighthouse, windmill, great tree)
   placed so at least one is visible from every approach and closes a street view.
3. **Main road:** enters from the direction of real traffic (the trade road, the gate, the
   docks), passes or grazes the centre, exits the other side. Slight curves and a width change
   at the square.
4. **Secondary streets:** branch from the main road at T-junctions every 9-20 blocks.
5. **Alleys:** fill the interior of long blocks; 1-3 wide, often stepped, sometimes covered.
6. **Edge road / ring:** where the wall once stood or where the fields begin.

## Layout patterns

| Pattern | Where it fits | How to build it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Organic / crooked | Medieval, fantasy, hillside, old core | Roads follow contours and property lines; no straight run over ~12; T-junctions; irregular block shapes |
| Grid | Colonial, Roman, modern, flat land, reclaimed | Blocks 15-25 by 25-40; break with one diagonal, a park, or terrain |
| Radial | Fortress towns, capitals, shrine towns | 4-8 roads from the centre + 1-2 ring roads; wedge-shaped blocks |
| Linear / ribbon | River banks, coasts, mountain passes, trade roads | One main road with a single row of plots each side, widening at the centre |
| Cluster | Farm hamlets, nomad camps, mining camps | 3-6 building groups around shared yards, linked by paths |
| Terraced | Steep slopes | Streets along the contours, stairs across them, retaining walls as the town's texture |

Most real settlements are hybrids: an organic old core, a grid extension, and a ribbon along
the road out of town. Mixing patterns by era is the fastest way to a plausible plan.

## Road construction

- Layers: base (coarse dirt / gravel), surface (dirt path, gravel, cobble, stone bricks,
  packed mud - by era and wealth), edge (slabs, walls, grass verge), wear (10% partner block
  in patches of 2-4).
- Crown and gutters on important streets: centre 1 block higher, or a slab strip at each side.
- Walkways: slab strips 1-2 wide along main streets in wealthy towns only.
- Junction treatment: widen by 1-2 blocks, change the surface pattern, add a well, a tree, a
  lantern post or a shrine.
- Stepped streets on slopes: 2-3 stairs then a 2-3 block landing; add a handrail (walls or
  fences) on the drop side.
- Bridges and fords where roads meet water - see
  `../../minecraft-organic-shapes/references/arches-and-bridges.md`.
- Lighting: lanterns on fence/wall posts every 8-10 blocks, offset to one side, plus brighter
  clusters at junctions and gates. Keep every walkable block at light level 1+ for spawn
  safety.

## Street enclosure

The ratio of building height to street width sets the mood:

| Height : width | Feel | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1:2 or wider | Open, rural, modern | Village edges, suburbs, plazas |
| 1:1 | Comfortable town street | Most streets |
| 2:1 | Tight, urban, dramatic | Old cores, alleys, market lanes |
| 3:1+ | Canyon, oppressive | Slums, fantasy cities, dwarven streets |

Overhangs (jettied upper floors), arches across the street, bridges between buildings and
awnings make tight streets feel deliberate rather than cramped.

## Squares and public space

- Every square needs: a hard centre object (well, fountain, statue, tree, market cross),
  stalls or benches at the edges, a change of paving, and a dominant facade.
- Market stalls: 3x3 to 5x5, fence posts + slabs/trapdoors for the awning, banners for colour,
  goods in item frames and barrels, a stall keeper's stool.
- Smaller public spaces: widened junctions, a churchyard, a green with a tree, a dock head, a
  garden behind a wall with a gate.
- Avoid one giant square - two or three differently sized spaces make a town feel explored.

## Walls, gates, defences

- Wall thickness 2-4 blocks with a walkway on top and merlons/crenellations; height 5-9.
- Towers every 20-40 blocks and at every corner and gate; gates 3-5 wide with a gatehouse,
  portcullis look (iron bars), and a widened road inside and outside.
- The wall should follow the terrain and step with it; break the line with towers and a
  collapsed section for age.
- Outside the wall: a ditch, a cleared glacis, suburbs that grew anyway, and the road fanning
  into tracks.
- Harbour: quay wall of stone with wooden piers, cranes (logs + chains + trapdoors), bollards,
  crates, moored boats, a lighthouse or a beacon tower at the entrance.

## Transition to nature

A settlement never ends with a line. Beyond the last plot put, in order: yards and gardens,
fields and pastures with irregular boundaries, orchards, a barn or two, tracks fading into the
landscape, then wild terrain. Keep some trees inside the settlement so it does not look
clear-cut.
