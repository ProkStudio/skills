# Floors, walls, ceilings

Surfaces carry 80% of the impression. Get them right before any prop is placed.

## Floors

Recipe: one base block (60%), one texture partner (30%), one accent (10%), plus a rug or
border to zone the room.

- **Patch, don't sprinkle.** Place the partner block in irregular patches of 2-5, never a
  checkerboard, never uniform noise.
- **Zone with material.** Walkway in stone, sitting area in planks, work zone in polished
  stone; or keep one material and change the pattern.
- **Borders** read as intentional: one ring of slabs, stairs or a darker block along the walls,
  or a 1-block inset line 1 block away from the wall.
- **Slab floors** let you sink fixtures half a block (baths, cauldrons, hoppers, campfires) and
  give a shallow step at thresholds. A slab floor plus a full-block hearth is an instant level
  change.
- Good pairings: oak planks + stripped oak + bamboo mosaic; stone bricks + cobbled deepslate +
  polished andesite; dark oak + spruce + mud bricks; smooth quartz + calcite + white concrete;
  tuff bricks + polished tuff + chiseled tuff (1.21).
- Patterned floors for grand rooms: 2x2 or 3x3 tiles with a contrasting joint block, a diagonal
  band across the axis, or a central medallion under the chandelier.

## Walls in three tiers

1. **Base course / wainscot** (1-2 blocks high): darker or heavier - stone, deepslate,
   mud bricks, stripped logs, or the wall block with a slab/stair cap.
2. **Field** (the bulk): the calm material, 2-3 blocks of the same value, different texture.
3. **Trim / cornice** at the ceiling line: slabs, stairs upside-down, trapdoors, walls, or a
   stripped-log band. Skip this and the wall reads as unfinished.

More rules:

- **Two-block-thick interior walls** where the plan allows. Each room gets its own finish, and
  the thickness hides lighting, niches, shelves and chimney flues.
- **Vertical rhythm:** pilasters, log posts or quoins every 4-6 blocks, aligned with the beams
  above. Structure should be readable indoors too.
- **Niches and recesses:** push 1 block into the wall for a bookshelf, a shrine, a bed alcove
  or a wash basin. Depth is cheaper than decoration.
- **Wall art placement:** paintings and banners at eye level (bottom edge at y+2 for 2-tall
  pieces), not floating near the ceiling.
- Textured plaster look: white/light concrete or terracotta field + stripped wood frames
  (half-timbering) with the frame following actual structure lines.

## Ceilings

Never leave a flat plane of the same block as the floor above. Pick one:

- **Exposed beams:** logs or stripped logs every 3-4 blocks spanning the short direction,
  with planks between. Add trapdoors or fence posts as brackets where beams meet walls.
- **Coffers:** a grid of beams with 2x2 or 3x3 recessed panels; put hidden light in the recess.
- **Trapdoor soffit:** trapdoors in the top of the wall ring create a shadow line and a
  "finished" edge for cheap.
- **Vault (stone rooms):** stairs stepping inward from both sides, meeting in a slab ridge;
  for a barrel vault run it the length of the room, for groin vaults cross two barrels.
- **Drop ceiling (modern):** a slab plane one block below the structural ceiling, with
  recessed strips of light and a different frame material at the perimeter.
- **Open truss (halls, workshops):** king-post trusses every 5-7 blocks, made of logs and
  fences, leaving the roof underside visible.

Colour rule: darker ceilings recede and make rooms feel taller; bright ceilings close in.
Use the darkest tier of the palette above, the lightest on the floor, mid on the walls - or
invert it deliberately for a modern gallery feel.

## Transitions

- Floor to wall: base course or a slab/stair skirting. Never plank into plank.
- Wall to ceiling: cornice tier. A stripped-log band works in every rustic palette.
- Room to room: threshold material change 1 block wide in the doorway, plus a trapdoor or
  stair frame around the opening.
- Level changes: 2-3 steps with a landing beat any single 1-block drop; stairs blocks plus a
  slab give a half-step.

## Openings from the inside

- Frame every window with full blocks so panes connect; use 2-wide openings when you want a
  glass surface, 1-wide only for arrow slits and vertical accents.
- Add depth: recess the glass 1 block, put a slab sill inside, trapdoor shutters outside.
- Interior windows (between rooms, or onto a hall) make small builds feel layered - use
  panes, bars, or an open arch with a fence railing.
- Curtains: banners on the sides, or wool with a trapdoor valance; matching the accent tier.

## Common surface mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| One block per surface | 3 blocks per tier, patched irregularly |
| Checkerboard floor | Patches of 2-5, or a proper tile pattern with a joint block |
| No cornice | Slab/stair/trapdoor band at the ceiling line |
| Ceiling = floor material | Give the ceiling its own structure and darker tone |
| Wall art floating high | Eye level, grouped, aligned with furniture below |
| Thin 1-block interior walls everywhere | Thicken the main dividing walls to 2 |
