# Build types

Type-specific programmes. Style comes from `styles/`, form from
`massing-and-silhouette.md`; this file covers what each type actually needs.

## Contents

- Houses and player bases
- Castles and fortifications
- Villages, towns, cities
- Farms and tech builds
- Interiors

## Houses and player bases

Programme before form: entry, storage, crafting, sleeping, and one signature room. Sketch
them as rectangles, then wrap the massing around them - houses designed shell-first end up
with unusable interiors.

- Storage wants a wall; windows want the view. Put storage on the blind facade.
- A player base also needs: a portal room, enchanting, brewing, farm access, and a landing
  or dock. Give each a real room, not a corner.
- Functional blocks (hoppers, droppers, observer lines) go behind panelling with an access
  hatch - a trapdoor or a removable block in a visible, remembered spot.
- Signature room ideas: double-height hall, library with a mezzanine, glass observatory,
  greenhouse, forge, bath, map room.
- Outbuildings multiply character cheaply: shed, well, oven, drying rack, woodpile, stable,
  outhouse, garden wall.

## Castles and fortifications

- Curtain wall: 3+ thick (2 walkway + 1 parapet), or 2 thick for small keeps. Thin walls read
  as fences.
- Crenellation rhythm: 2 blocks merlon, 1 gap, repeating; or 1-1 for a finer look. Keep the
  rhythm consistent on one wall and vary it between structures.
- Batter: slope or step the bottom 2-4 blocks of the wall outward. This single move makes
  walls read as fortification rather than fencing.
- Towers: at least 3 different heights and 2 different plans (square, round, D-shaped,
  polygonal). Corner towers project *out* from the wall so they can cover it.
- Gatehouse: two flanking towers, a recessed gate, portcullis (iron bars on pistons or just
  bars), murder holes, a bridge or causeway, and a barbican if the scale allows.
- Machicolations: overhang the parapet 1 block on brackets with gaps between them.
- Arrow slits: 1x2 openings, wider inside than outside, irregularly spaced.
- Inner ward: keep (dominant mass), hall, chapel, barracks, kitchen, stables, well, workshops.
  Leave a courtyard - a solid block of buildings reads as a city, not a castle.
- Ground: castles command terrain. Put it on a ridge, spur or island and let the walls follow
  the contours instead of forming a perfect rectangle.

## Villages, towns, cities

- **Layout first**: roads before plots. Main street 4-5 wide, secondary 3, alleys 1-2.
  Curve them; a grid only for planned colonial/modern settlements.
- Anchor the centre with a public building and an open space (well, market, square, church,
  hall). Density decreases outward; gardens and yards fill 20-40% of the area.
- **Plot variety**: run the variation engine per building. Adjacent buildings must differ in
  at least footprint shape, roof type, and height; they should share the palette base while
  swapping accents.
- Repeat 3-4 house "archetypes" with rolled variations rather than 20 unique designs - that
  is how real villages read: family resemblance, not clones.
- Fill the space between buildings: fences, hedges, gardens, woodpiles, carts, stalls,
  laundry lines, lamp posts, wells, notice boards, bridges, drainage.
- Roads need destinations - a gate, a mill, a mine, a dock, a field. Dead-end roads look
  generated.
- Verticality: a church spire, bell tower, windmill or water tower gives the settlement a
  skyline.

## Farms and tech builds

- Wrap the mechanism in architecture: barn, mill, greenhouse, warehouse, workshop, silo,
  tower. The shell is a building, not a hull.
- Never break the mechanic for looks. Before covering anything check: spawn platform light
  levels and mob caps, water flow lines, redstone signal paths, hopper chains, observer sight
  lines, and required air gaps.
- Access matters: hatches, ladders, a maintenance walkway, and a labelled control panel (levers
  + signs + item frames) near the entrance.
- Give it industrial language: chimneys with smoke (campfire below), pipes (waxed copper +
  cauldrons), chains, hoppers as funnels, iron bars as railings, hanging lanterns, a loading
  bay with rails and minecarts.
- Make the function visible - a wheat farm looks like a barn with a granary, an iron farm
  looks like a foundry.

## Interiors

Exterior-only builds break the illusion the moment the player walks in.

- **Ceilings**: exposed beams every 2-3 blocks (stripped logs, fences for slender beams),
  coffers, a slab layer, or a vaulted stair ceiling. A flat plank ceiling is the interior
  equivalent of a box.
- **Floors**: change the pattern per zone - planks in living areas, stone in kitchens and
  entries, carpets as rugs with a border pattern. Mix 2-3 blocks as with exterior texturing.
- **Walls**: change material at chair-rail height (1-2 blocks up); panel with trapdoors,
  add bookshelves, hung banners, item frames, or a plaster infill between beams.
- **Furniture** from stairs (chairs, sofas with signs as armrests), trapdoors (tables,
  cabinet doors), barrels and chests (storage, counters), lecterns (desks), cauldrons (sinks),
  campfires and smokers (hearths), beds with banner canopies, pots and composters.
- **One focal point per room**: hearth, window seat, bed alcove, bookcase wall, stair,
  workbench cluster.
- **Lighting**: hidden or in-fiction - lanterns on beams, candles on tables, chandeliers of
  chains + lanterns, sea lanterns behind trapdoors, fireplaces. Never bare glowstone.
- **Circulation**: leave 2-3 blocks of walking space; doors need clear approach; stairs need
  headroom (3 blocks above the step line).
- **Windows**: every interior window must exist on the outside, at the same y level. Check
  from outside after building the interior.
- **Verticality**: at least one double-height space or a mezzanine in anything larger than a
  cottage.
