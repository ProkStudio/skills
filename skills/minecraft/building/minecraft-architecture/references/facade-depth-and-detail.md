# Facade depth and detail

Detail on a flat plane still looks flat. Depth first, decoration second.

## Contents

- The depth pass
- Vertical zoning
- Window recipes

## The depth pass

Before any decoration, offset the wall plane in and out at least twice per facade:

| Move | How | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plinth / base course | 1-2 blocks tall, pushed out 1, different material | grounds the build |
| Pilaster / buttress | 1-block column pushed out, every 3-5 blocks | rhythm, shadow lines |
| Recessed bay | pull a 3-5 wide section in by 1 | reads as a shaded panel |
| Oriel / bay window | push a 2-3 wide window box out by 1, support with brackets | strong focal point |
| Jetty / overhang | upper floor 1 wider than lower, on upside-down stairs | medieval, adds shadow |
| String course | slab or stair band at each floor line | horizontal scale |
| Cornice | upside-down stairs + slab at the top of the wall | finishes the wall |
| Chamfered corner | replace corner blocks with stairs or walls | softens the box |

Shadow is the point: every offset creates a dark line that does the visual work.

## Vertical zoning

Read a wall as three bands, each with its own treatment:

1. **Base** (1-2 blocks) - heaviest, roughest material; moss, gravel transition to ground.
2. **Main** (floor heights) - the palette base + openings + structure.
3. **Crown** (1-3 blocks) - cornice, frieze, brackets, parapet or eave.

Material changes at band boundaries, never mid-band without a trim line.

## Window recipes

Anatomy: jamb (sides), sill (bottom), lintel (top), glazing, and optional shutters/box.

- **Cottage**: full-block jambs, upside-down stair sill, log or stair lintel, pane recessed
  1 block, spruce trapdoors as shutters.
- **Stone / castle**: 1x2 or 1x3 opening, wall block or pane inside, stone stair lintel and
  sill, deeper 1-2 block reveal for thick walls.
- **Gothic / arched**: 3 wide, top course stepped with stairs and slabs into an arch, glazing
  in panes or tinted glass.
- **Asian**: shoji grid - white/bone-block infill framed by dark oak trapdoors or fences.
- **Modern**: full glass blocks floor-to-ceiling, thin frames of polished deepslate or
  black concrete, no sills - or corner glazing with no post at all.
- **Industrial**: iron bars + panes grid, 4+ wide, with a transom band above.

Rules: openings 2+ wide when a glass surface is wanted, jambs are full blocks so panes and
bars connect, and window rhythm follows the bay grid. Recess glazing at least 1 block - flush
glass is the flattest thing you can do to a wall.

## Entrances

The entrance must be the loudest point of the facade:

- Widen: 2-3 blocks for houses, 3-5 for halls; double doors, or a fence gate for utility.
- Deepen: recess the door 1-2 blocks, or push out a porch 2-3 deep with its own roof.
- Frame: taller jambs, an arch or pediment, a change of material, lanterns both sides.
- Approach: 2-3 steps up, a landing, a path that widens toward the door.
- Above: a sign board, hanging sign, banner, balcony, bay window, or a small gable.

## Timber framing and structure expression

- Verticals (logs or stripped logs) every 2-4 blocks, matched by a sill beam at the bottom
  and a plate beam at the top of each floor.
- Diagonals only exist as steps - stairs stepped 1 over / 1 up, or a stair-and-slab braced
  corner. Keep them short (2-4 blocks) so the steps read as a brace, not a staircase.
- Infill in a lighter material with a different texture (calcite, bone block, white terracotta,
  mud bricks, plaster-like smooth stone).
- Exposed purlins: fences or stripped log ends poking out under the eave overhang.
- Corbels and brackets: upside-down stair, or stair + slab, under every overhang. Overhangs
  with no visible support look wrong even in Minecraft.

## Greebles

Small objects that sell scale and inhabitation. Use in clusters of 2-3, not evenly spread:

- Structural: chains, lightning rods, iron bars, trapdoor shutters, ladders, scaffolding.
- Domestic: barrels, decorated pots, cauldrons, composters, lanterns, candles, flower pots,
  bee nests, hanging signs, item frames with tools, banners, bells.
- Soft: leaves and vines at corners, moss carpet, pink petals, hanging roots, azalea bushes,
  big dripleaf, glow lichen.
- Trade/industry: anvils, grindstones, smokers, blast furnaces, stonecutters, hoppers,
  lecterns, cartography tables, brewing stands - these also say what the building does.

## Restraint rules

- Two thirds of every surface stays calm. Detail concentrates at entrances, corners,
  openings, eaves, and the base.
- Do not detail what the player will never see - spend the effort on the approach facade.
- One focal point per facade. Two competing focal points equals none.
- If a facade still looks empty after a full depth pass, the problem is usually missing
  structure (columns, floor lines) rather than missing decoration.
- Repetition needs a break every 5-7 bays: a wider bay, a balcony, a chimney, a different
  window, a passage through the building.
