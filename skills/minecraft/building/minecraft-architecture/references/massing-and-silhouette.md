# Massing and silhouette

Form is decided before material. A great palette cannot rescue a bad mass, and a great mass
survives a mediocre palette.

## Footprint

Never a single rectangle. Pick one and commit:

| Shape | Good for | Bonus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L | houses, farms, inns | inner courtyard, roof valley |
| T | chapels, halls, gatehouses | strong entrance axis |
| U | manors, barracks, stables | enclosed yard |
| Cross | churches, towers, keeps | 4 facades, 4 gables |
| Core + satellites | cottages, bases, workshops | organic growth look |
| Terraced / stepped | slopes, cliffs, cities | terrain integration for free |

Rules of thumb: keep the main volume's proportions between 1:1.3 and 1:2. Attachments are
30-60% of the main volume's footprint. Offset attachments so they do not share a centreline
with the main mass unless the style is formal.

## Three-mass rule

Split the build into:

- **Primary** (~60% of volume) - sets the style and the roof language.
- **Secondary** (~30%) - wing, annex, tower, service block, second storey over part of the plan.
- **Tertiary** (~10%) - porch, bay window, chimney stack, balcony, stair turret, dormers,
  lean-to shed.

Each mass gets its own roof, and at least one of them gets a *different* roof type. Two
masses of identical size read as a mistake - make the difference obvious (at least 1.4x).

## Vertical break

At least one element rises 1.5x above the general roofline: tower, gable end turned 90
degrees, chimney, belfry, ridge lantern, silo, mast, smokestack. Without it the build reads
as a slab regardless of detail.

Stagger floor heights between masses so the eaves do not all sit on one y level. A 1-2 block
offset between eave lines is enough.

## Proportion and scale anchors

| Element | Cottage | Manor / hall | Keep / monumental |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Interior floor height | 4 | 5 | 6-8 |
| Wall thickness | 1 | 1-2 | 2-3 |
| Entry width | 1-2 | 2-3 | 3-5 |
| Entry height | 2-3 | 3-4 | 5-7 |
| Window opening | 1x2 | 2x2 / 2x3 | 3x4+ |
| Trim depth | 1 | 1-2 | 2-3 |

Scale detail with the build. Monumental builds need fewer, bigger moves: a 3-block cornice,
a 5-block arch, a 7-wide stair. Repeating 1-block trim on a 40-block wall reads as noise.

## Parity and rhythm

- Symmetric gables and hipped towers need **odd** widths (5, 7, 9, 11) so the ridge or apex
  lands on a block, not a seam.
- Even widths force a 2-block ridge - fine for gambrel/mansard, wrong for a helm roof.
- Bays repeat every 3-5 blocks. Use an A-B-A or A-B-B-A pattern across a facade, and break
  the pattern once (wider bay, balcony, oriel) to avoid a barracks look.
- Corners want emphasis: quoins, a log column, a 1-block pilaster, or a chamfer (cut the
  corner block and replace it with stairs).

## Silhouette test

Before detailing, imagine the build as a flat black shape against the sky:

1. Is the outline a rectangle? Fail - add masses, roof variety, a vertical break.
2. Are there at least three distinct height steps? If not, stagger them.
3. Does the top edge have interest (chimney, dormer, finial, crenellation, antenna)?
4. Does the outline read from 40+ blocks away? Details vanish at range; only shape survives.

Also check the top-down outline. A 4-sided plan is the same failure seen from above.

## Common failures

- **Extrusion** - a nice floor plan pulled straight up with no setbacks or overhangs.
- **Symmetry everywhere** - mirror only formal builds (churches, palaces, gatehouses).
- **Two identical wings** - make one larger, shorter, or differently roofed.
- **Detail instead of form** - decoration is not a substitute for massing.
- **One-block porch** - a porch must be at least 2 deep to read as a space.
- **Flat top on a tall build** - add a crown (spire, terrace, parapet with corner turrets).
