# Roofs

The roof is 30-50% of the visible build. Choosing it by default (45 degree stair gable) is
the single most recognisable mark of a generated build.

## Choose by span

| Span (width) | Works | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3-6 | gable, shed, hip, thatch dome | mansard, sawtooth |
| 7-12 | gable, half-hip, Dutch gable, hip, gull wing, bell-cast | plain gable above 12 |
| 13-18 | gambrel, mansard, half-hip, monitor, clerestory, hip with dormers | single-pitch gable |
| 19+ | mansard with parapet, sawtooth, monitor, flat with terrace, vaulted, multi-ridge | anything with one unbroken slope |

A gable above ~12 blocks wide becomes an enormous blank triangle - break it with dormers, a
hip, a pitch change or a second ridge.

## Type catalogue

| Type | Shape | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gable | two slopes, triangular ends | default cottage; needs support above 12 wide |
| Hip | four slopes meeting at ridge or apex | towers, formal builds; square plan gives a pyramid |
| Half-hip (jerkinhead) | gable with the top corner hipped | rustic, breaks a big gable cheaply |
| Dutch gable (gablet) | small gable sitting on a hip | manors, temples, Asian roofs |
| Gambrel | steep lower pitch, shallow upper | barns, inns; buys attic volume |
| Mansard | near-vertical lower, almost flat upper | city blocks, palaces; needs dormers + parapet |
| Gull wing | shallow lower, steep upper | fantasy, whimsical, wide overhangs |
| Bell-cast | main pitch flaring out at the eave | Asian, French, fairy-tale |
| Saltbox | asymmetric ridge, one long slope | organic growth, lean-to additions |
| Shed / skillion | single pitch | modern, sheds, stacked cascades |
| Butterfly | two slopes meeting in a valley | modern, brutalist, water collection look |
| Monitor | raised central section with side glazing | halls, industrial, markets |
| Clerestory | ridge split with a glazed vertical band | churches, workshops |
| Sawtooth | repeated asymmetric ridges, glazed one side | factories, warehouses |
| Helm (Rhenish) | four gables + pyramid between | towers, keeps; odd widths, 7x7 sweet spot |
| Flat + parapet | flat with a raised border | modern, industrial, desert, city |
| Terrace / green | flat with garden, turf or pergola | nordic, hobbit, modern |
| Dome / onion | rotated rings stepping in | temples, palaces, wizard towers |
| Thatch / turf | thick organic mass | rustic, nordic, fantasy villages |

Mix types on one build: main mass gets the primary type, wings and turrets get contrasting
ones. This alone kills most of the sameness.

## Pitch construction

- **45 degrees (1 up per 1 in)** - plain stair rows. Clean but overused; if used, break it
  elsewhere (eave, ridge, dormers, material).
- **Shallow (1 up per 2 in)** - alternate full block and slab, or stairs with a slab step
  between rows. Reads calmer and bigger.
- **Steep (2 up per 1 in)** - full block + stair pairs, or stair on top of a block, repeated.
  Best for Nordic, fantasy and church roofs.
- **Very steep (3 up per 1 in)** - vertical block column with stair caps, for spires.
- **Compound** - steep for the first 3-5 rows, shallow above (gambrel/mansard feel), or the
  reverse for gull wing.
- **Curved** - shift the pitch every 2-3 rows (steep, medium, shallow) for a bell-cast or
  Asian sweep; add a final upside-down stair at the eave tip for the upward flick.

Roof thickness: for spans above ~10, make the roof 2 layers (stairs over slabs, or stairs
over full blocks) so eaves and gable ends read as a thick, solid roof rather than paper.

## Breaking the stair look

Apply at least two per build:

1. Change pitch partway up.
2. Add dormers, or a gablet at the ridge.
3. Overhang the eave 1-2 blocks with an underside (slabs or trapdoors as soffit) and a
   darker fascia board.
4. Cap the ridge with a different block - slabs, opposite-facing stairs, a fence/chain run,
   or a lantern line.
5. Two roof tones - e.g. deepslate tile stairs mixed 70/30 with polished deepslate stairs,
   or a darker lower course; place the variation in patches, not stripes.
6. Chimneys and stacks that pierce the slope, with flashing (a slab collar) at the junction.
7. Hips and valleys from a non-rectangular footprint.
8. A skylight, roof window, or glazed ridge band.

## Eaves, ridges, gable ends

- **Eave**: overhang 1 on cottages, 2 on Asian/Nordic/fantasy. Support with upside-down
  stairs, brackets (stair + slab), or exposed fence purlins under the overhang.
- **Fascia**: the vertical edge of the overhang in a darker block sharpens the roof outline.
- **Ridge**: never leave the top row as two facing stairs alone on large builds - add a ridge
  course of slabs, a row of opposite stairs, a beam, or finials at both ends.
- **Gable end**: return the roof line with upside-down stairs so it reads as a barge board.
  Fill the tympanum with something - timber framing, a round window, a vent, a hoist beam,
  a hanging sign, or a contrasting material.
- **Verge**: a 1-block step of the gable wall above the roof (a parapet gable) is a strong
  alternative used in brick and stone styles.

## Dormers and roof furniture

- Dormer width 1-3; keep the same roof type as the main roof (or a hip on a gable roof).
- Spacing: rhythm matching the windows below, never centred alone on a wide roof.
- Dormer roof pitch should be equal or steeper than the main roof.
- Roof furniture that adds life: chimneys (2x2 for large builds, with a cap), weather vanes,
  lanterns, chains, banners, hoist beams, bird nests (leaves + decorated pot), skylights,
  small platforms, antennae or pipes on industrial builds.

## Flat roofs

Always give a border in a *different* block: a slab/stair parapet, an upside-down stair
gutter, or a wall run with lanterns. Then occupy the surface - terrace, garden, tanks, AC
units, pergola, seating. A bare flat roof reads as an unfinished box. Light it or cover the
walkable surface (slabs, carpets, pressure plates) so mobs do not spawn there.

## Diagonal and round roofs

- Stairs only face 4 directions, so **diagonal roofs are built from slabs and full blocks**,
  stepped 1 in / 1 up for 45 degrees or 2 in / 1 up for a shallow slope.
- Round and dome roofs: build the circle first (use a block-circle reference), then step each
  ring in by 1 and up by 1-2; smooth the profile with slabs on the flatter rings.
- Helm roofs: place slabs under the main stair rows so the two gable directions meet without
  gaps, and keep all stairs facing only two opposite directions.

## Checklist

- [ ] Type chosen from the catalogue for this span, not by habit.
- [ ] At least two "break the stair look" measures applied.
- [ ] Eave overhang with soffit and fascia.
- [ ] Ridge treated.
- [ ] Gable ends not blank.
- [ ] Roof material differs in value from the walls (usually darker).
- [ ] Secondary masses have a different roof type from the primary.
