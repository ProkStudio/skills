# Modern minimal

Geometry, cantilevers, glass, and restraint. Depth comes from volumes sliding past each
other, not from decoration. Sloppy shapes have nowhere to hide.

## Signature

- Intersecting rectangular volumes, one clearly cantilevered or offset.
- Flat roofs with thin parapets; strong horizontal slab lines.
- Full-height glazing in large uninterrupted panels.
- Three materials maximum, crisp joints, no blending.

## Massing

- 2-3 boxes: one horizontal base, one rotated or shifted upper volume, one vertical core
  (stair, chimney, entry tower).
- Cantilever 2-4 blocks over an entry, a pool or a slope - the signature move.
- Floor slabs project 1 block past the glass to read as horizontal bands.
- Asymmetry everywhere; large glass on the view side, near-solid walls on the street side.
- Floor heights 5-6 for a luxury feel; double-height living volume in anything large.

## Roofs

Flat + parapet (default), shed/skillion for a modernist accent, butterfly for a statement,
terrace/green roof for warmth.

- Parapet: 1 block of a contrasting material, or an upside-down stair gutter, or a glass
  balustrade of tinted glass panes with iron trapdoor railings.
- Occupy the roof: terrace with a pergola of stripped logs and glass, planters, a pool, a
  skylight strip, roof plant (AC units of copper grate + cauldrons).
- Overhangs are thin - a slab layer with a shadow gap below the fascia.

## Palette

| Tier | Blocks |
| --- | --- |
| Base (light) | white concrete, smooth quartz, quartz block, calcite |
| Base (dark) | black concrete, polished deepslate, polished blackstone, deepslate tiles |
| Neutral | light gray concrete, smooth stone, polished andesite, gray concrete |
| Warm accent | stripped birch / stripped oak / stripped spruce cladding, honeycomb-toned copper |
| Glazing | glass blocks, tinted glass, gray/light gray stained glass, iron bars for screens |
| Landscape | polished andesite paving, gravel, moss block, azalea, bamboo, water |

Pick either light-dominant with dark trim, or dark-dominant with warm wood. Never a 50/50
light/dark split - one must clearly lead.

## Details

- Wood-slat cladding: alternate stripped log and plank columns, or use bamboo mosaic; runs
  vertically on one volume only.
- Shadow gaps: recess a slab line where two materials meet instead of butting them together.
- Railings: iron trapdoors, tinted glass panes, or a single wall run - pick one, use it
  everywhere.
- Lighting: hidden strips (sea lanterns or copper bulbs behind trapdoors and under slabs),
  end rods as downlights, glowstone under carpets on terraces.
- Entry: a recessed 3-wide portal, a floating slab canopy, a single warm-wood door wall.
- Pool, reflecting pond, or a water channel that continues the building's geometry.
- Landscape: gravel and paving grids, planting in rectangular beds, 1-2 sculptural trees.

## Interiors

Open plan with zones defined by floor material and ceiling height, a feature wall (dark stone
or wood slats), a fireplace or TV wall as the focal point, hidden cove lighting, furniture
from slabs, stairs and trapdoors in the same palette, glass balustrades on the mezzanine.

## Mistakes

- Panes instead of glass blocks - panes read as divided old-fashioned windows here.
- Too many materials; four or more kills the style.
- Flat roof with no parapet and nothing on it.
- Symmetrical box with a flat roof and no cantilever (that is just a box).
- Quartz-white everything with no dark or warm counterweight.
- Ignoring the site - this style needs paving, water and planting geometry to look finished.
