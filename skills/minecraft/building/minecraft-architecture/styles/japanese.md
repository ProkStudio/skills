# Japanese / East Asian

Calm surfaces, dark heavy roof, visible post-and-beam structure, and a designed relationship
with the garden. Texture blending is minimal - crispness is the point.

## Signature

- Sweeping roof with a big overhang (2-3 blocks) and an upward flick at the eave.
- Post-and-beam frame: dark columns on a stone base, light infill panels between.
- Raised floor - the building sits on a 1-2 block podium with a step or veranda.
- Deep veranda (engawa) running along one or two sides.

## Massing

- Low and horizontal; length 2-3x the height. Verticality only in pagodas and gate towers.
- Modular bays of 3 blocks between columns, repeated evenly (this style *allows* regularity).
- Multi-tier composition: main hall + lower wings connected by covered walkways.
- Pagodas: odd number of tiers, each tier 80% of the one below, roofs dominating.

## Roofs

Primary: hip-and-gable (irimoya) or bell-cast hip. Gate: simple gable with huge overhang.
Pagoda: stacked bell-cast hips.

- Build the pitch in 3 zones: steep near the ridge, medium, shallow at the eave, then a final
  upside-down stair at the tip for the flick.
- Corners: add a 1-block "wing" of stairs projecting diagonally so hips curve up at the ends.
- Ridge: a thick 2-block course (polished deepslate / dark prismarine) with end caps.
- Roof thickness 2 layers minimum; the underside of the overhang gets a soffit of slabs plus
  fence or stripped-log purlins.
- Roof material must be clearly darker than the walls.

## Palette

| Tier | Blocks |
| --- | --- |
| Roof | deepslate tiles, dark prismarine, blackstone, polished basalt, dark oak stairs |
| Structure | dark oak log, stripped dark oak, spruce log, bamboo planks for lighter frames |
| Wall infill | bone block, calcite, white terracotta, smooth quartz, white concrete (modern temples) |
| Base / podium | stone bricks, andesite, cobbled deepslate, tuff (1.21+) |
| Accent | red nether brick or crimson (shrines, torii), copper, cherry planks (1.20+) |
| Garden | gravel, white concrete powder for raked gravel, moss, azalea, cherry leaves, bamboo, lily pads |

## Details

- Shoji screens: bone block or calcite framed by dark oak trapdoors; or glass panes framed by
  fences for translucency.
- Lattice: iron bars or fences in a bay, backed by white blocks.
- Torii gates: two columns + two crossbeams, red or dark wood, standing free on the approach.
- Stone lanterns: stone brick base + slab + lantern + slab cap.
- Engawa veranda: slab floor 1 block above the ground, fence balustrade, posts at bay spacing.
- Garden as part of the build: raked gravel field, koi pond with lily pads, stepping stones,
  a single specimen tree (cherry or azalea), bamboo cluster, moss-covered rocks.
- Covered walkways connecting masses, roofed and on posts.

## Interiors

Tatami zoning (moss carpet or yellow/green carpets in 2x1 patches), low furniture from slabs
and trapdoors, sliding panels, a tokonoma alcove with a pot or banner, paper lanterns, beam
grid ceiling, minimal clutter. Empty space is intentional.

## Mistakes

- Symmetrical roof with no overhang - the whole style collapses without the overhang.
- Heavy texture blending on walls (keep infill panels clean and flat).
- Jungle or acacia wood; use dark oak/spruce for structure, bamboo for light elements.
- Building flush on the ground with no podium.
- Bright quartz white everywhere; bone block and calcite read warmer and better.
- Forgetting the garden - in this style the exterior space is half the build.
