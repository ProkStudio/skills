# Elven / natural fantasy

Slender, flowing architecture that grows out of the landscape instead of clearing it. Every
line is curved, every structure is tall and thin, and trees are part of the building.

## Signature

Tall pointed arches, verdigris roofs, spiral forms, buildings woven into trees and cliffs, and
gold filigree on pale wood.

## Massing

- **Vertical and slender.** Towers 5-9 blocks wide and 20-40 tall, connected by bridges and
  walkways at several levels rather than one ground plan.
- Nothing sits on a flat pad: every structure grips a cliff, straddles a stream or wraps a
  trunk. See `../references/terrain-integration.md`.
- Organic silhouette rules: no straight run longer than 7 blocks, every plan curve made from
  three or more different chord lengths, and every tower tapering as it rises.
- Circulation is part of the massing: curved external stairs, suspended bridges of fences and
  slabs, spiral ramps around trunks.
- Group buildings in odd numbers at different heights, never in a row.

## Roofs

- Steep and tall - 1.5:1 or steeper - with a flared, curved eave that turns outward in the
  last two blocks.
- Conical and onion roofs over round towers; layer tables in
  `../../minecraft-organic-shapes/references/domes-and-spheres.md`.
- **Verdigris is the signature material**: oxidized cut copper stairs and slabs (1.17+, all
  four oxidation stages have stairs and slabs, plus waxed twins to freeze the stage). Before
  1.17, use prismarine and dark prismarine.
- Finish each spire with an end rod, a lantern or a gold block finial, and let leaves overhang
  the eave so the roof merges with the canopy.

## Palette

| Tier | Blocks |
| --- | --- |
| Base ~45% | Pale wood: birch planks, stripped birch, or pale oak (1.21.4+) for the whitest version; calcite and diorite for stone elements |
| Secondary ~25% | Oxidized and weathered cut copper (1.17+) for roofs and panels; prismarine as a pre-1.17 substitute |
| Nature ~20% | Moss block and carpet, azalea leaves, flowering azalea, vines, glow lichen, mangrove roots (1.19+), rooted dirt |
| Accent ~10% | Gold blocks and gold trim, amethyst blocks and clusters (1.17+), sea lanterns |
| Glass | Light blue, white and lime stained glass panes in tall narrow windows; tinted glass (1.17+) for dark slots |
| Light | Sea lanterns behind glass, end rods, glow berries (1.17+), lanterns on chains, amethyst-lit niches |

## Details

- Pointed arches, always taller than wide: 3 wide by 5-6 tall, made with stairs and a single
  keystone block; run them in colonnades of 5-7.
- Filigree: a 1-block band of gold, end rods, iron bars or chains tracing every roof edge and
  window head. Thin, continuous, everywhere - this is what separates elven from generic
  fantasy.
- Tree integration: build the trunk first as a real tree with mangrove roots (1.19+) or logs
  fanning at the base, then let platforms and walkways spiral around it. The tree must look
  like it came first.
- Water features: a channel following the contour, waterfalls off the walkway edges, a still
  pool of prismarine with sea lanterns beneath it.
- Planting inside the build: moss carpet on walkways, azalea and flowering azalea in every
  corner, cave vines with glow berries hanging from the underside of bridges.
- Curves everywhere: no 90-degree corner should be left sharp - chamfer it with stairs.

## Interiors

Tall narrow rooms with exposed curved rib beams of stairs, pale plank floors, a central spiral
stair, and window slots reaching floor to ceiling. Furniture is light and organic: fence-post
tables, leaf-covered planters, a loom and enchanting table as focal objects, banners in pale
greens and golds. Light softly from sea lanterns behind white glass and glow berries, level
8-11, uneven and dappled.

## Mistakes

- Straight, blocky towers with a fantasy palette pasted on. The geometry is the style.
- Flat ground around the build; if the terrain is not doing something, the build fails.
- Heavy dark timber and thick walls - keep everything slender.
- Skipping the filigree band, which is what reads as "elven".
- Trees that were clearly added afterwards to fill gaps.
