# Mediterranean

Whitewashed masonry, terracotta roofs, deep shade and hard light - Greek islands, southern
Italy, coastal Spain. Cubic volumes stacked on a slope, not composed on a flat pad.

## Signature

White walls, low-pitch orange roofs with heavy eave courses, arched loggias, flat roof
terraces with pergolas, blue doors and shutters.

## Massing

- Stacked cubes: 2-3 boxes of different heights and depths, offset 1-3 blocks, sharing walls.
  Roof terraces on the lower cubes serve the upper ones.
- Build **into a slope**; stepped terraces with retaining walls are the native condition. See
  `../references/terrain-integration.md`.
- Wall height 4-5 blocks per floor, openings 2-3 wide because the climate wants shade.
- A loggia or arcade of 3-5 arches along the sunny facade, recessed 1 block.
- Optional stair tower or bell gable to break the height line.

## Roofs

- Shallow pitch, 1:2 or 1:3, hipped or simple gabled, always with a 1-2 block eave course of
  upside-down stairs plus a slab so the roof reads as heavy tiles.
- Terracotta colour with a usable variant set: bricks and brick stairs, mud bricks and mud
  brick stairs (1.19+), or granite with polished granite. Plain terracotta has no stairs or
  slabs before 26.3 and still has none in any colour of glazed form - never promise a
  terracotta stair roof.
- Mix the roof field in patches of 2-4 blocks: roughly 60% bricks, 25% mud bricks,
  15% granite.
- Make half the roofs flat terraces instead: stone slab deck, low wall parapet, pergola of
  stripped logs and fences with vines and glow berries (1.17+).

## Palette

| Tier | Blocks |
| --- | --- |
| Base ~60% | White concrete, white terracotta, calcite (1.17+), bone block, smooth quartz for the brightest walls |
| Secondary ~30% | Smooth sandstone and cut sandstone for stucco patches, stone bricks for plinths and retaining walls |
| Accent ~10% | Bricks, mud bricks (1.19+) and granite for roofs; light blue and blue terracotta or concrete for doors, shutters and domes |
| Trim | Stripped oak and jungle logs, trapdoor shutters, fence pergolas, iron bars as window grilles |
| Glass | Small openings, panes on the shaded side only; often open with bars instead of glass |
| Light | Lanterns, candles, terrace campfires, sea lanterns behind quartz for pool light |

## Details

- Arches everywhere: 3-5 block spans, rise tables in
  `../../minecraft-organic-shapes/references/arches-and-bridges.md`.
- Vary the whitewash: patch calcite, white concrete and bone block, and expose stone bricks
  near the ground where the render has failed.
- Exterior stairs climbing a blank wall to a roof terrace, with a low wall balustrade.
- Planting: olive grove from spruce logs with low-trimmed azalea or oak leaves, cypress
  columns of dark oak logs and spruce leaves, bougainvillea from pink petals (1.20+) and
  flowering azalea leaves over a pergola.
- Water: a cistern or fountain in prismarine with waterlogged stairs, a stepped harbour edge.
- Shade is a material - every facade needs a recessed loggia or a deep reveal.

## Interiors

Cool and bare: stone or terracotta floors, white plaster walls, exposed stripped-log beams,
arched internal doorways, few small windows. Cauldrons, barrels and a smoker for the kitchen;
a long table of slabs on barrels. Light at 7-9 from lanterns in wall niches. Add one tiled
feature: a band of blue glazed terracotta at floor level, rotated so the pattern runs
continuously.

## Mistakes

- A flat pad with a white box on it. The style lives on slopes and terraces.
- Steep roofs. Above 1:1 this becomes northern European.
- Uniform pure-white walls with no exposed plinth.
- Promising terracotta stairs or slabs, which do not exist.
- Forgetting the flat terraces; a Mediterranean cluster is half roof, half deck.
