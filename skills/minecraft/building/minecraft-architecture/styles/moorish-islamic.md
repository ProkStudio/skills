# Moorish / Islamic

Geometry, water and shade. Plain outside, dense inside: a blank street wall opens into a
courtyard with a pool, arcades, tilework and a dome.

## Signature

Horseshoe arches, a courtyard with a cross-axial water channel, geometric tile bands, pierced
screens, a dome on a square base, slender minarets.

## Massing

- **Design the courtyard first**, then wrap rooms around it. The courtyard should be at least
  9x9 and can be half the total footprint.
- Exterior walls are tall, flat and nearly windowless; all the richness faces inward.
- Arcades on two to four sides of the courtyard, one storey with a gallery above.
- One vertical accent only: a minaret or stair tower, square in plan, 3x3 to 5x5, rising well
  above the roofline with a small pavilion cap.
- Roofline is mostly flat with parapets; the dome sits over the main hall, never centred on
  the courtyard.

## Roofs

- Flat terraces with a 1-block parapet of walls and a crenellated or stepped edge.
- Domes: build the square base, corbel the corners in two or three stepped rings to make an
  octagon, then dome from there. Layer tables in
  `../../minecraft-organic-shapes/references/domes-and-spheres.md`.
- Dome skin in prismarine, dark prismarine, cut copper (1.17+) for verdigris, or blue-green
  glazed terracotta for a tiled dome; cap with a gold block finial.
- Small pyramidal roofs of stairs over the minaret and gate pavilions.

## Palette

| Tier | Blocks |
| --- | --- |
| Base ~60% | Smooth sandstone, cut sandstone, sandstone; swap to calcite (1.17+) or white concrete for a whitewashed Andalusian version |
| Secondary ~25% | Quartz block and smooth quartz for carved surfaces, stone bricks for plinths |
| Accent ~10% | Glazed terracotta in blue, light blue, white and cyan for tile bands; gold blocks sparingly |
| Water | Prismarine, prismarine bricks, dark prismarine for pools and channels |
| Screens | Iron bars, glass panes, copper grate (1.21+), dark oak trapdoors for mashrabiya |
| Light | Lanterns, candles (1.17+), sea lanterns under water, end rods inside the dome |

## Details

- **Glazed terracotta is the key block.** Its pattern rotates with `facing`, so a band of the
  same colour can be laid to form continuous geometry. Place it by hand, in bands 1-2 blocks
  high at dado level and around doorways - never as a whole wall.
- Horseshoe arches: cut a 3-wide opening, corbel the springing inward by one block on each
  side with stairs, then arch above so the opening is wider than its throat.
- Muqarnas approximation: three or four receding rings of upside-down stairs and slabs in the
  soffit of each arch and under the dome.
- Jali screens: alternate iron bars and glass panes, or use copper grate (1.21+) as a solid
  field, in the gallery openings instead of glass.
- Water is architecture: a 1-wide channel crossing the courtyard on both axes, a shallow
  central pool of prismarine, and a fountain of waterlogged stairs. Keep it still and clean.
- Planting is formal: four quadrants, symmetrical, with cypress columns and potted flowers.
- Repeat one geometric motif at three scales: in the tile band, in the screens, in the
  parapet crenellation.

## Interiors

High ceilings, arched openings, tiled dado with plain plaster above, carpets on stone floors,
and a fountain in the main hall. Ceiling in dark oak with a coffered pattern of slabs and
stairs. Light indirectly: sea lanterns behind quartz, candles in wall niches, level 8-11.
Avoid furniture clutter - the surfaces carry the decoration.

## Mistakes

- Windows all over the exterior. The outside is meant to be closed.
- Glazed terracotta used as a full wall texture; it becomes noise. Keep it to bands.
- A dome sprouting straight from a square wall with no corbelled transition.
- Symmetry abandoned inside the courtyard - this style is strictly axial.
- Dry courtyards. Water on both axes is the plan's whole idea.
