# Brutalist

Raw concrete mass, honest structure and aggressive geometry. This style needs scale: below
about 20 blocks in any dimension it reads as an unfinished grey box.

## Signature

Heavy monolithic volumes, deep window reveals, exposed board-formed concrete, cantilevers,
and a separate service tower.

## Massing

- Compose from **few, huge volumes** - three or four at most - intersecting at right angles.
- **Cantilever something.** An upper volume projecting 3-6 blocks over the base, unsupported,
  is the fastest way to read as brutalist.
- Pilotis: lift part of the mass on 2x2 or 3x3 columns with open space beneath.
- A vertical service core - stair or lift tower, blank walls, taller than the rest.
- Repetitive module: a structural bay every 4-6 blocks, expressed as projecting fins or deep
  recesses over the whole facade.
- Asymmetry is fine here, but every offset should be at least 3 blocks so it reads as
  deliberate.

## Roofs

- Flat, with the parapet as a thick 1-2 block band that overhangs slightly to cast a shadow
  line.
- Roofscape is functional: plant rooms, a cooling block, skylight strips of tinted glass
  (1.17+), and a stair head box.
- Optional inverted pyramid or stepped section where the top volume grows outward as it rises.

## Palette

| Tier | Blocks |
| --- | --- |
| Base ~55% | Gray concrete and light gray concrete as the concrete field |
| Texture ~25% | Smooth stone, stone, polished andesite and andesite patched into the field so the grey is never flat |
| Shadow ~10% | Deepslate, polished deepslate, deepslate tiles (1.17+), or cobblestone for a coarser aggregate |
| Warm relief ~10% | Stripped spruce or dark oak for soffits, window frames and door surrounds |
| Glass | Tinted glass (1.17+) for ribbon windows; gray stained glass elsewhere; iron bars for grilles |
| Light | Sea lanterns recessed into soffits, end rods in reveals, redstone lamps for entry signage |

No saturated colour outside a single accent - a red door, an orange handrail, nothing more.

## Details

- **Board-formed texture**: lay the grey field in vertical stripes 1-2 blocks wide, alternating
  gray concrete, light gray concrete, smooth stone and polished andesite. Add a horizontal
  seam of stone slabs every 4-5 blocks to imitate formwork lifts.
- **Deep reveals.** Every window sits 1-2 blocks back from the face; this style is about wall
  thickness, and a flush window kills it instantly.
- Fins and brise-soleil: a grid of walls or stone fences across a sunny facade, 1 block
  proud, spaced every 2 blocks.
- Expressed circulation: a stair run visible through a full-height glazed slot in the core.
- Ground condition: raw concrete plinth, gravel and coarse dirt planting beds, concrete
  planters of rooted dirt (1.17+) with unkempt vegetation and glow lichen staining.
- Weathering is welcome - patch a few stone and cobblestone blocks below window sills as
  water staining.

## Interiors

Leave the concrete visible: grey walls, exposed beam soffits of slabs, dark plank or gray
concrete floors, and one warm element (bookshelves, a spruce wall) per room. Big open spaces
with a double-height void and a cantilevered internal balcony. Light coldly and unevenly,
level 10-12, with light sources hidden in soffit recesses.

## Mistakes

- Too small. Brutalism at cottage scale looks like a bunker by accident.
- One uniform concrete colour with no patching or seams.
- Flush windows and thin walls.
- Decorative trim, arches or pitched roofs - none of them belong.
- No cantilever or overhang, leaving a stack of plain boxes.
