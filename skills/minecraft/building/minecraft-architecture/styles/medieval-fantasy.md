# Medieval fantasy

Not historical medieval - exaggerated proportions, leaning timber, heavy roofs, hand-made
feel. The style lives on **overhangs and roof mass**.

## Signature

- Roof is 40-50% of the visible height. If the roof looks small, the build is not this style.
- Upper floors jetty out 1 block over the lower ones, on visible brackets.
- Stone below, timber and plaster above (the ground floor takes the weather).
- Nothing perfectly straight - wobbly rooflines, added-on sheds, crooked chimneys.

## Massing

- Footprint L or core + satellites; 2-3 masses with staggered eave lines.
- 2-3 floors, each one 1 block wider than the one below on at least one side.
- Vertical break: stair turret, chimney stack, or a gable turned 90 degrees.
- Narrow plots in towns (5-7 wide), pushed together, sharing walls.

## Roofs

Primary: steep gable (2 up per 1 in) or half-hip. Secondary masses: shed, gablet, or conical
turret. Thatch and gambrel for farm buildings.

- Pitch 2:1 minimum; 3:1 for fairy-tale exaggeration.
- Overhang 2 blocks with fence purlins under it and a dark fascia.
- Ridge: opposite-facing stairs + a lantern or finial at each end.
- Dormers on anything wider than 10.
- Thatch: hay bales with dried kelp block patches, edges softened with a slab course and
  trapdoors as a comb at the ridge.

## Palette

| Tier | Blocks |
| --- | --- |
| Base (stone) | cobblestone, mossy cobblestone, andesite, stone bricks, cracked stone bricks, tuff bricks (1.21+) |
| Base (plaster) | white terracotta, calcite, bone block, diorite, smooth stone |
| Secondary (timber) | spruce log, stripped spruce log, dark oak log, spruce planks, spruce trapdoors |
| Accent (roof) | deepslate tiles + polished deepslate stairs, dark oak stairs, blackstone, red nether brick for wealthy roofs |
| Trim | polished andesite, stone stairs, cobbled deepslate |
| Light | lanterns, candles, campfires, torches on brackets |

Board-and-plaster facades: 70% plaster infill, 30% timber frame. Wealthier buildings swap
plaster for brick or more carved stone.

## Details

- Timber framing with short stair-stepped braces in each bay.
- Window boxes: upside-down stair sill + flower pot or pink petals; trapdoor shutters.
- Hanging signs on iron chains over doors (shop signs), banners on poles, bells.
- Barrels, crates (chests), cauldrons, anvils, grindstones outside workshops.
- Chimney stacks in cobble/brick, 2x2 on big builds, with a slab cap and a campfire below
  for smoke.
- External stairs to upper doors, wooden galleries, laundry lines of chains.
- Arched stone doorways: 3 wide, stairs stepping into the arch.

## Interiors

Exposed beams, plank floors with a stone hearth zone, a fireplace as the focal point,
bookshelves, barrels, a ladder or spiral stair, low light from lanterns and candles.

## Mistakes

- Shallow roof - instantly reads as generic.
- Oak everywhere (too orange); use spruce/dark oak as the structural wood.
- Perfectly symmetric facades and evenly spaced identical windows.
- Cobblestone as the only stone (blend it).
- Timber frame drawn as a flat grid on every wall with no relation to floors or bays.
- Clean, moss-free ground floor.
