# Classical antiquity (Greek and Roman)

Order, podium, column, entablature. The style is a rule system: get the proportions right and
the build works with almost no decoration.

## Signature

- Everything stands on a **podium** of 2-4 steps that wraps the whole building.
- Columns on a strict rhythm, with a horizontal entablature band on top - never arches sitting
  directly on columns (that is Roman late/Byzantine; if you want arches, pair them with piers).
- Low roof pitch (1:3 to 1:4) with a triangular pediment on the short facade.
- Strong horizontal banding: podium, column zone, entablature, cornice, roof.

## Massing

- Greek temple: rectangle, ratio about 1:2 (e.g. 11x23), colonnade all round (peripteral) or on
  the front only.
- Roman temple: deeper podium (4-6 blocks), columns at the front, closed walls at the sides.
- Villa: courtyard (peristyle) plan - open square surrounded by colonnades, rooms behind.
- Civic: basilica as a long hall with internal colonnades; baths as intersecting vaulted volumes.
- Column count on the facade is even (4, 6, 8) so the centre is an opening, not a column.

## Roofs

Primary: shallow gable at 1:3 or 1:4. The pediment triangle is the face of the building.

- Tile courses: brick stairs or granite stairs laid in rows, with a slab ridge and an
  overhanging cornice of 1-2 blocks on slabs.
- Antefixes: a small upright block (stair or wall block) at each tile course end along the eave.
- Acroteria: a decorative block (chiseled quartz, flower pot, end rod) at the pediment apex and
  both lower corners.
- Roman vaulted volumes (baths, basilica) use barrel vaults instead - see
  `../../minecraft-organic-shapes/references/arches-and-bridges.md`.

## Palette

| Tier | Blocks |
| --- | --- |
| Base (marble look) | calcite, smooth quartz, quartz block, diorite, bone block, white terracotta |
| Secondary | smooth sandstone, sandstone, polished diorite for floors and podium |
| Accent (roof) | bricks + brick stairs, granite + polished granite, red terracotta bands |
| Trim | quartz stairs and slabs, quartz pillar (columns), chiseled quartz block (capitals) |
| Light | lanterns on chains, campfire braziers, candles, gold block inlays |

Columns: quartz pillar for fluting (axis `y`), or bone block for a warmer stone. Capitals:
chiseled quartz block, or a stair ring under a slab for a Doric flare.

## Details

- Column proportions by order: Doric 1 wide x 7-8 tall, Ionic 1 wide x 9-10 tall with a
  slab-and-stair scroll capital, Corinthian 1-2 wide x 10-12 tall with a leaf capital
  (azalea leaves or moss carpet ring under the abacus).
- Intercolumniation: 2-3 blocks of air between column centres. Tighter reads Egyptian, wider
  reads modern.
- Entablature in three bands: architrave (1 block plain), frieze (2 blocks with chiseled
  panels or a repeating motif), cornice (1 block of slabs projecting 1 out).
- Pediment: fill the triangle with a slightly recessed panel and put a single motif at the
  centre (gold block, chiseled quartz, banner).
- Podium steps: 3 blocks wide on the entrance side, 1-2 on the others, using slabs and stairs.
- Paving: polished diorite / smooth quartz / andesite in a large geometric pattern; add a
  mosaic with terracotta colours in a villa.
- Water: rectangular impluvium pool in the courtyard, fountains, aqueduct arcade approaching
  the site.
- Sculpture: statues on 2-block plinths between columns, bronze (copper) or marble (quartz).

## Interiors

- Cella (temple interior): one tall room, a statue on an axial podium, light only from the door
  so the statue is lit from the front.
- Villa: rooms open onto the peristyle, mosaic floors, plastered walls (white terracotta) with a
  red or ochre dado band, low couches from stairs, braziers.
- Ceilings: coffered grids of 3x3 recesses using slabs with a gold or contrasting centre block.

## Mistakes

- Odd number of facade columns, so a column blocks the entrance axis.
- Columns going straight into the ground with no podium and no base.
- No entablature: columns touching the roof directly makes the build read as a pergola.
- Steep roof pitch - anything over 1:2 stops being classical.
- Pure white concrete for marble; it is too flat and too cold. Calcite and smooth quartz with
  diorite variation read far better.
- Decorating every surface. Classical is calm surfaces with concentrated ornament.
- Forgetting weathering. For a ruin, remove column drums, crack the pediment and add moss,
  vines and fallen blocks on the ground - see
  `../references/palette-and-texture.md` for the weathering rules.
