# Western frontier

An American boomtown thrown up fast out of cheap timber: tall false fronts hiding small
sheds, a continuous boardwalk, and a dusty street that does the composition.

## Signature

False-front facades with a square parapet, a covered boardwalk on posts, shallow tin roofs
behind the fronts, hand-lettered signs, and a dirt street.

## Massing

- **The street is the build.** Two rows of narrow buildings, 7-11 blocks wide each, sharing
  party walls, facing a 9-13 block street. A single western building alone looks unfinished.
- Each building is small - one or two storeys - but the front wall continues 2-3 blocks above
  the actual roof to make a tall flat facade.
- Vary the parapet profile along the row: flat, stepped, gabled, curved. No two neighbours
  should match.
- Continuous boardwalk 2-3 blocks deep along both rows, roofed by a shed awning on posts,
  with the deck raised 1 block above the street.
- Landmarks to punctuate the row: a water tower on stilts, a church with a small spire, a
  livery stable with a large opening, a windmill.

## Roofs

- Shallow shed or gable roofs, 1:3 or flatter, deliberately hidden behind the false fronts -
  only visible from the side or from above.
- Corrugated tin look: iron trapdoors laid flat, or waxed weathered copper and cut copper
  slabs (1.17+) for rusted metal. Pre-1.17, use iron blocks sparingly or stick to planks.
- Awning over the boardwalk: a one-block-deep slope of spruce stairs and slabs on stripped
  log posts every 3 blocks, with a plank soffit below.
- Add failure: a missing board, a patched panel in a different wood, a sagging awning corner.

## Palette

| Tier | Blocks |
| --- | --- |
| Base ~50% | Spruce, oak and dark oak planks, mixed per building so each shopfront differs |
| Structure ~20% | Stripped spruce and oak logs for posts, beams and railings |
| Masonry ~15% | Sandstone, smooth sandstone and cut sandstone for adobe and chimney stacks; bricks for the bank |
| Metal and roof ~10% | Iron trapdoors, iron bars for the jail, copper (1.17+) for rusted roofs |
| Ground | Dirt path, coarse dirt, gravel, with a few grass patches at the edges only |
| Light | Lanterns on posts, campfires, candles (1.17+) in windows |

## Details

- **Signage matters more than the buildings.** Use hanging signs (1.20+) projecting from each
  facade, plus a big painted sign band across the false front made of dark planks with a
  contrasting wood border. Pre-1.20, signs on fences plus banners.
- Double swing doors for the saloon: two trapdoors side by side above a 2-wide opening, or a
  pair of fence gates.
- Windows: 2x2 glass panes with a plank lintel and a trapdoor shutter; a few boarded up with
  crossed stair and slab planks.
- Boardwalk detail: railing of fences, hitching posts, a water trough of a cauldron or
  waterlogged slab, barrels and crates of hay bales, a rocking chair of stairs and trapdoors.
- Street furniture: wagon (stairs, barrels, fence-post wheels of walls), a well, a wanted
  board, tumbleweed of dead bushes.
- Let the ground be scruffy: gravel ruts down the street centre, coarse dirt at the edges, a
  puddle of waterlogged slabs.

## Interiors

Saloon: bar of spruce stairs with barrels behind, piano of note blocks and black carpet, round
tables of fence posts and pressure plates, a staircase to a landing. General store: shelves
of barrels and item frames, a counter, a stove of a furnace and a chimney. Keep floors plank,
walls plank, ceilings beamed - all one material family. Light warm at 8-11 from lanterns and
candles.

## Mistakes

- One isolated building. Build at least four in a row.
- Identical facades. Mix wood, parapet shape and sign for each shopfront.
- A visible tall roof - the false front exists precisely to hide it.
- No boardwalk, or a boardwalk flush with the ground.
- Neat lawns and clean paths; this street should look dry and worn.
