# Chinese imperial

A timber hall on a stone podium under a deep, double-curved tile roof. The roof is 50-60% of
the visual mass, and everything below it is a repeating bay system.

## Signature

Upturned eaves, bracket clusters under the overhang, red columns on a white stone podium,
glazed tile roofs, and strict bay repetition.

## Massing

- Three stacked zones: **podium** (2-3 blocks, wider than the hall), **body** (one storey of
  columns and screens), **roof** (as tall as the body or taller).
- Odd bay count - 3, 5, 7 or 9 bays wide - with a column every 3 blocks and the centre bay
  used as the entrance.
- Rectangular plan, long axis facing the approach. Complexes are courtyard-based: gate,
  forecourt, main hall on the axis, side galleries.
- Balustrade around the podium: walls with a slab cap, stairs at the centre bay only.
- For pagodas, repeat the roof zone 3-7 times, shrinking each storey by 1 block per side.

## Roofs

The hard part, and where most builds fail.

- Hipped roof with a **double curve**: start the slope steep near the ridge (1:1), then flare
  it out to nearly flat at the eave (1:3), and turn the last block of each corner *upward*
  with a stair and a fence post or end rod.
- Overhang 3-4 blocks past the wall - deeper than any European style.
- **Bracket clusters (dougong)**: a visible band under the eave built from upside-down stairs
  and slabs alternating with the column line. Never let the roof meet the wall directly.
- Ridge treatment: a raised ridge beam of slabs with a finial at each end and a small
  ornament on the corner ridges.
- Tile colour depends on the version, because saturated blocks lacked stairs for most of the
  game's history:
  - Any version: deepslate tiles and stairs (1.17+) for grey-black tiles, prismarine or dark
    prismarine for blue-green, red nether brick and nether brick for dark red, purpur for
    violet.
  - **26.3+**: concrete stairs and slabs exist in every colour, so a true imperial yellow or
    green glazed roof is finally possible. See
    `../../../versions/minecraft-version-history/references/timeline-2026.md`.

## Palette

| Tier | Blocks |
| --- | --- |
| Podium ~25% | Smooth quartz, quartz block, calcite (1.17+), diorite; stone bricks for the retaining face |
| Columns and frame ~20% | Red: crimson planks (1.16+), red terracotta, red concrete, red nether bricks. Dark timber: dark oak and spruce for beams |
| Roof ~35% | Deepslate tiles (1.17+), prismarine, dark prismarine, nether brick, purpur; concrete stairs and slabs from 26.3 |
| Accent ~10% | Gold blocks for finials and door furniture, chiseled quartz for carved panels |
| Screens | Dark oak trapdoors, fences, glass panes for lattice windows |
| Light | Lanterns on the eave line, red or white candles, end rods for indirect light |

## Details

- Lattice windows: dark oak trapdoors in a grid, or alternating fences and glass panes over
  the full bay width. Walls are screens, not masonry.
- Paint the structure, not the surface: red columns, dark beams, white podium, and keep wall
  infill plain.
- Hang a lantern or two per bay from the eave using chains (1.16+).
- Approach matters: a paved axial path, a pair of guardian statues or drum stones, a gate
  pavilion with its own small roof.
- Roof corner detail: stair, then a fence post one block up and out, topped with an end rod
  or a hanging lantern - this single move sells the upturned eave.
- Repetition is the ornament. Identical bays, identical brackets, identical lanterns.

## Interiors

One tall open hall, timber columns in the same rhythm as the facade, a coffered ceiling of
dark oak slabs and stairs, stone or dark plank floor with a central carpet runner, and a
raised platform at the far end. Keep furniture sparse and symmetrical: two braziers, a low
table, screens along the side walls. Light at 9-12 from lanterns hung between columns.

## Mistakes

- A single-curve 45-degree roof. The flare at the eave is the style.
- A roof that lands directly on the wall with no bracket band.
- Even bay counts, which leave no central entrance.
- Skipping the podium - the hall must sit on a base, never on grass.
- Promising glazed terracotta or wool stair roofs before 26.3; those variants did not exist.
